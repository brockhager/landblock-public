# Governance Executive Brief

This brief explains Landblock's governance model in simple institutional terms.

Landblock does not replace courts or governments.
Courts decide land.
Governments decide policy.
Landblock governance is limited to the neutral infrastructure and standards
used to record those decisions.

---

## The Core Separation

There are two very different categories of authority:

- Sovereign authority over land outcomes
- Governance of protocol rules, standards, and infrastructure stewardship

Landblock governance belongs only in the second category.

That means Landblock governance does not decide:

- Who owns land
- Which claims are legally valid
- How a court should rule
- What law applies in a jurisdiction

Those decisions remain with courts, governments, and other recognized
authorities.

---

## What Landblock Governance Does

Landblock governance may help decide:

- How public-facing standards evolve
- How schemas are updated
- How upgrades are proposed and reviewed
- How neutrality is protected
- How maintainers and operators remain accountable

This is governance of infrastructure, not governance of land itself.

---

## Why This Matters

When governance roles are not clearly defined, institutional risk increases.
External parties reasonably want to know:

- Who controls changes to the system
- What constrains that power
- How continuity is maintained over time
- Whether any private actor can override the rules informally

An explicit governance model answers those questions better than informal,
person-dependent decision-making.

---

## The Institutional Position

Landblock should be understood as neutral infrastructure stewardship.

It is closer to:

- A standards body
- A steward of public-interest infrastructure
- A charter-based governance process

It is not:

- A court
- A sovereign authority
- A substitute for public institutions

---

## Plain-Language Statement

"Landblock does not replace courts or governments. Courts decide land.
Governments decide policy. Landblock governance only stewards the neutral
infrastructure and standards used to record those decisions."

---

## Bottom Line

The governance model is defensible only if the boundary remains strict:

- Public institutions decide land outcomes
- Landblock governance stewards protocol rules and infrastructure processes
- The system gains legitimacy by separating sovereign authority from technical
  stewardship
