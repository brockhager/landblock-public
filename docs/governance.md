# Landblock Governance

## Governance As Stewardship

Landblock uses collective governance to steward shared infrastructure over long
time horizons.

This governance model is about accountability, continuity, and neutrality in
how the platform evolves. It is not designed to replace public institutions,
and it is not a claim of legal authority.

Landblock's implementation is private. Its governance philosophy is public.

---

## Why Collective Governance Matters For Public Infrastructure

Land systems require trust, continuity, and clear responsibility.

Landblock's governance approach is informed by a practical lesson: when
decision-making is concentrated in a small number of individuals, long-term
institutional risk increases. Risks include unclear authority, weak continuity,
and limited checks on power.

Collective governance is used to reduce those risks by making authority:

- Explicit
- Constrained
- Documented
- Accountable over time

This does not remove accountability. It formalizes accountability.

---

## Clear Separation Of Authority

The most important principle is role separation.

### Courts, Governments, And Recognized Authorities Decide

- Land ownership and legal validity
- Dispute outcomes and legal rulings
- Jurisdictional rules and policy

### Landblock Governance Decides

- Protocol and software evolution
- Public standards and schema updates
- Upgrade pathways and stewardship processes
- Neutrality and continuity constraints

Landblock governance does not adjudicate land claims.
It does not override courts or governments.
It does not act as a legal authority.

Landblock may record assertions and rulings from recognized sources, but it
does not create those outcomes.

Governance exists above infrastructure decisions, not above law.

## Current Governance Structure

- **Founding Multisig**: Live on Polygon Amoy, handles initial setup and transitions.
- **Constitution**: Ratified, defines governance principles and constraints.
- **DAO Transition**: Planned migration to Aragon DAO with neutrality constraints to ensure protocol rules govern infrastructure, not land ownership.

---

Landblock currently explores governance tooling on Aragon because it provides
mature, well-audited governance components suitable for early protocol
stewardship.

In this context, Aragon is treated as a practical governance environment for
the current phase of development.

### Why Landblock May Execute On Kadena Later

Over time, Landblock may support governance execution on Kadena or other chains
to align with long-term goals around scalability, operational cost, and
infrastructure strategy.

These execution choices are infrastructure decisions, not authority decisions.
The chain is an execution environment, not the source of legitimacy.

---

## Governance Portability

Landblock is designed around governance portability.

Governance rules are defined first in human-readable constitutional and policy
documents. On-chain components are implementations of those rules.

This order matters because it allows governance execution to migrate between
platforms without losing continuity, accountability, or institutional clarity.

In short:

- Documents define authority and boundaries
- Implementations execute defined processes
- Migration of tooling does not change legal or institutional roles

---

## Long-Term Neutrality And Institutional Adoption

For governments, institutional partners, and researchers, the central question
is not whether governance uses modern tooling.

The central question is whether authority is clear and bounded.

Landblock's governance model aims to support adoption by maintaining a strict
boundary:

- Public institutions retain authority over land outcomes
- Landblock governance stewards neutral technical infrastructure

This separation supports long-term neutrality while reducing reliance on any
single operator, company, or informal decision center.

---

## Summary: Governance That Enables, Not Replaces

Landblock uses collective governance to maintain infrastructure responsibly over
time.

It does not claim sovereign authority.
It does not decide land truth.
It does not replace courts or governments.

Its role is narrower and intentional: steward open, neutral infrastructure so
recognized authorities and institutions can interact with durable systems.
